# Tell Me About Yourself: LLMs Are Aware of Their Learned Behaviors

## Metadata
- **Authors**: Jan Betley, Xuchan Bao, Martín Soto, Anna Sztyber-Betley, James Chua, Owain Evans
- **Year**: 2025 (arXiv v1, January 2025)
- **Source**: arXiv preprint (2501.11120)
- **DOI/URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11120 (code and datasets: https://github.com/XuchanBao/behavioral-self-awareness)

## Summary
The paper introduces "behavioral self-awareness": the ability of a chat LLM to accurately describe a behavioral policy it was fine-tuned into, without in-context examples or chain-of-thought, even though the fine-tuning data never describes that policy. The authors test this across risk-seeking economic choices, the Make Me Say persuasion game, and insecure code generation, and extend it to backdoored models and multi-persona training. Self-description is reasonably reliable for most behaviors, but backdoor triggers can only be elicited free-form with a reversal-training augmentation.

## Key Findings
- GPT-4o and Llama-3.1-70B fine-tuned only on multiple-choice answers that consistently pick the risky option describe themselves as "bold", "aggressive" and "reckless" (Fig. 2, Fig. 3). The same fine-tuning on risk-averse answers yields "cautious"-type self-descriptions.
- Self-reported risk levels correlate with measured behavior across fine-tuning runs with different learning rates and seeds (Fig. 4; reported r values roughly 0.45 and 0.67). The authors describe this as "to some extent" and note it may reflect a common cause rather than introspection.
- Models fine-tuned on Make Me Say dialogues (never containing the codeword) can state their codeword and goal. Across most evaluations they beat GPT-4o (Fig. 6), though the "Write a function" and "Describe the word" evaluations are noisier.
- Models fine-tuned on insecure code report lower code security and lower self-reported alignment with human values (Table 2).
- Backdoored models sometimes report that their behavior depends on a particular feature of the user message, and can pick out their real trigger from fake triggers (Fig. 8, Fig. 9). They cannot output the trigger in free-form text by default, which the authors attribute to the reversal curse.
- Reversal-augmented training (swapping user and assistant turns in a fraction of the data) lets the model articulate its trigger ("username: sandra") about 30% of the time when prompted with vague descriptions such as "misaligned behavior" (Fig. 11). Without the augmentation, the rate is 0%.
- With many-persona fine-tuning (other personas answered with default GPT-4o behavior), the model reports the altered behavior for the default persona only, and this generalizes to out-of-distribution personas (Appendix B.2, Fig. 15).
- Only 32 training examples for 1 epoch are enough to induce both the risk behavior and its self-report (Appendix C.1.10, Table 28).

## Methodology
Supervised fine-tuning (OpenAI API for GPT-4o; LoRA rank 4 via Fireworks for Llama-3.1-70B) on datasets where the assistant consistently exhibits a latent policy, with explicit mention of that policy removed. Datasets were generated by GPT-4o with few-shot prompting and filtered for leaked keywords. Evaluation uses held-out questions in different formats (free-form, numeric scale, multiple-choice, two-hop tasks), with 10 paraphrases and about 100 samples each. Behavior is measured separately from self-report, so the two can be compared. Backdoors are inserted following Hubinger et al. (2024), and triggers are tested with multiple-choice and free-form probes. Note: no RL is used anywhere in the paper.

## Relevance to Proposal
**Score: 4**

The paper is relevant to the proposal's model-organism step: it shows that SFT-implanted policies, including goal-directed ones like Make Me Say, can be read out by asking the model directly. That is useful for checking whether a reward-seeking organism has actually internalized the target disposition, and for the SDF/salience extension. It does not address RL, learning speed, or whether reward seeking transfers to new environments better than instruction following, which is the proposal's central question.

## Limitations
- Authors state results are limited to three settings (economic choice, Make Me Say, insecure code) and to GPT-4o and Llama-3, with no scaling study.
- Backdoor articulation is weak: free-form trigger elicitation works only with reversal training and only about 30% of the time. The authors note that eliciting triggers without already knowing them is not yet demonstrated.
- Self-report faithfulness is "to some extent" with significant noise, and the paper does not establish whether the correlation reflects introspection or a common cause (Section 7).
- Many evaluations use GPT-4o as the judge or as the source of the baseline, and the Make Me Say "Describe the word" task depends on another LLM's guesses.
- Behaviors are instilled by SFT on synthetic data, which is far from the RL setting the proposal targets. The authors also note that GPT-4o safety filters limited how far the risk-seeking data could be pushed (Appendix C.4.1, footnote 5).
- The authors do not claim models are aware of behaviors in any mechanistic sense.
