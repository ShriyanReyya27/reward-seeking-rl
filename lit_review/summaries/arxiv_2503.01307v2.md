# Cognitive Behaviors that Enable Self-Improving Reasoners, or, Four Habits of Highly Effective STaRs

## Metadata
- **Authors**: Kanishk Gandhi, Ayush Chakravarthy, Anikait Singh, Nathan Lile, Noah D. Goodman
- **Year**: 2025
- **Source**: COLM 2025 (arXiv preprint 2503.01307, v2)
- **DOI/URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01307

## Summary
The paper asks why some base LLMs improve a lot under RL on the Countdown arithmetic game while others plateau (Qwen-2.5-3B reaches about 60% versus about 30% for Llama-3.2-3B under identical PPO training). The authors argue that four cognitive behaviors (verification, backtracking, subgoal setting, backward chaining) present in the initial policy determine whether RL can amplify useful reasoning, and they show that priming Llama with synthetic traces containing these behaviors, even incorrect ones, closes much of the gap.

## Key Findings
- Qwen-2.5-3B shows a qualitative shift around RL step 30 (longer responses, rising accuracy); Llama-3.2-3B shows little change under the same setup. Qwen's baseline rates of verification and backtracking are much higher than Llama's, and Llama-3.1-70B is still uneven on backtracking.
- SFT priming Llama on synthetic traces with backtracking (plus verification, subgoal setting, or backward chaining) produces large RL gains, roughly matching Qwen. RL amplifies some primed behaviors and suppresses others (e.g., backward chaining and subgoal setting in the all-strategies condition).
- Priming with empty chain-of-thought or length-matched placeholder tokens does not help (scores around 30-35%, close to base Llama). The authors read this as evidence that the behaviors, not extra tokens, are the causal ingredient.
- Priming with incorrect solutions that display the right reasoning behaviors gives performance comparable to priming with correct solutions.
- Continued pretraining on OpenWebMath rewritten to amplify these behaviors (about 8.3M tokens, classified and reformatted with Qwen-2.5-32B) lets Llama match Qwen's RL trajectory. The behavior-minimized control does not.
- Instruction-tuned Qwen and Llama (Appendix I) nearly saturate the Countdown score and show the behaviors emerging under RL, which the authors attribute to instruction-tuning data containing such traces.
- Transfer (Appendix H): the curated-pretraining and RL-on-Countdown models show higher rates of the behaviors on GPQA and MATH, but no significant accuracy gain on GPQA.

## Methodology
Base models Qwen-2.5-3B and Llama-3.2-3B are trained with PPO (VERL and TinyZero, 250 steps, 4 rollouts per prompt, reward 1 for correct, 0.1 format bonus). Behaviors are measured by GPT-4o-mini classifying reasoning traces (4 questions per trajectory, temperature 0), with inter-rater ICC3 of about 0.4 to 0.9 against Claude and two humans. Interventions are: (1) SFT priming on about 1,000 Claude-3.5-Sonnet traces per behavior condition, with system prompts that restrict which behaviors are used; (2) empty/placeholder CoT controls; (3) incorrect-solution priming; (4) continued pretraining on classifier-curated OpenWebMath with a behavior-minimized control. The work is empirical, with no formal model.

## Relevance to Proposal
**Score: 4**

The paper is a close precedent for the proposal's design: it runs identical RL on two base models and compares learning speed, uses SFT warm-starts before RL, and includes matched-compute control conditions (empty CoT, placeholder tokens). Its finding that behaviors in the training data matter more than answer correctness bears on whether a reward-seeking warm-start would change RL learning speed. It does not study reward seeking, reward hacking, or misaligned motivations, and its outcome variable is task accuracy on a capability task rather than generalization of a propensity across environments.

## Limitations
- Only Countdown is used for the main results. The authors note that priming was domain-specific and that the behaviors that matter may differ across tasks (e.g., coding, games).
- Only two 3B base models are compared, with one RL recipe. Seed variance and number of runs are not reported in the text reviewed.
- Behavior counts depend on a GPT-4o-mini classifier. Agreement with humans is moderate for backward chaining (ICC3 0.40-0.55 with humans).
- The behavior set is hand-picked and not exhaustive, as the authors acknowledge.
- Priming data is generated by Claude-3.5-Sonnet, and its correctness is only about 60-75% in some conditions, so "incorrect" traces are a specific kind of error.
- Claims of causality rest on interventions over a small number of conditions.
- No reward hacking, misspecified reward, or safety-relevant evaluation is included.
