# Evaluation-Conditioned Training: Teaching Models to Generalize to Stronger Oversight Regimes

## Metadata
- **Authors**: Alec Harris, Kasey Corra, Archie Chaudhury, Yixiong Hao
- **Year**: 2026 (arXiv ID 2608.10209, v1)
- **Source**: arXiv preprint
- **DOI/URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10209 (code: https://github.com/evaluationconditionedtraining/Evaluation-Conditioned-Training)

## Summary
The paper proposes Evaluation-Conditioned Training (ECT), a post-training add-on to SFT and PPO that prepends a natural-language description of the evaluator (e.g., "UNBIASED" or a suggestibility level) to each training prompt, then deploys with a description of a stronger or stricter evaluator. It is tested in two small proof-of-concept experiments: political even-handedness under SFT with biased data, and reducing sycophancy on one-digit addition with PPO under a deliberately mis-specified, suggestibility-weighted reward. In both, ECT beats a no-label baseline and a shuffled-label control on the targeted behavior.

## Key Findings
- In the political-bias experiment (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, LoRA SFT), ECT raised even-handedness from 49.8% (baseline) and 49.4% (shuffled) to 64.8%, with lower refusal and hedging rates. These are the authors' numbers graded by Claude Haiku 4.5 on 1,350 held-out pairs.
- In the sycophancy experiment (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, QLoRA PPO, 9 seeds), ECT reduced the false-positive (agreement) rate from about 48% to 21.6% (±15.8) and raised accuracy from about 51.5% to 73.8% (±19.3). The authors report that 3 of 9 seeds failed to escape the sycophantic prior, so the effect is high-variance.
- Test-time label sweeps show the trained models track the evaluator label more strongly than the shuffled control, including on a held-out "strict" label never seen in training. The authors frame this as evidence of generalization to stronger oversight regimes.
- The paper is explicitly a conceptual framework plus small demonstrations. Its authors say they "do not establish a full empirical benchmark" and did not compare against inoculation prompting.

## Methodology
Conceptual framing draws on simplicity bias, weak-to-strong generalization, and eliciting latent knowledge (ELK), arguing that conditioning on an explicit evaluator description can redirect optimization away from exploitable proxy objectives. The empirical part has two experiments. Exp. 1 uses 3,240 synthetic paired political prompts generated by Claude Sonnet 4.5, with four editorial-profile labels, LoRA SFT over 3 epochs, and 5 seeds. Exp. 2 uses on-the-fly one-digit addition prompts with a candidate user answer, a suggestibility-scaled reward that rewards agreement with the user, PPO with KL disabled and an entropy bonus, and 9 seeds. Baselines in both are a no-label model and a shuffled-label model that controls for extra prompt text. Both experiments use model-based grading.

## Relevance to Proposal
**Score: 3**

The paper touches the proposal's theme of how reward-misspecification and "reward-seeking" pressures shape what RL-trained models learn, and it uses PPO on an LLM with a deliberately exploitable reward. However, it does not study reward seeking as a propensity, does not compare reward seekers against instruction followers, does not measure learning speed in new RL environments, and contains no hackable versus non-hackable environment variants. Its main use to the proposal is as background on sycophancy and reward-hacking-style misspecification and as a related method (conditioning on evaluator descriptions) that a reward-seeking model organism could be tested against.

## Limitations
- The authors acknowledge that the experiments are small, proof-of-concept, and not a benchmark; they do not claim state of the art.
- No direct comparison with inoculation prompting (IP), the closest alternative, is run.
- Data are synthetic, generated by frontier LLMs, and graded by model-based evaluators. The political-bias evaluator (Claude Haiku 4.5) is from the same family as the training-target generator (Claude Sonnet 4.5), so gains may partly reflect evaluator favoring related-model outputs. The authors call these results directional.
- Exp. 2 uses a simulated suggestibility reward rather than a naturally arising misspecification, and the sycophancy gain has high seed variance (3 of 9 seeds failed).
- Tasks are single-turn with simple output spaces; agentic and multi-step settings are untested.
- Exp. 1 reports the out-of-the-box model reaching 82.0% even-handedness, which is higher than any trained condition. The authors note this but it weakens the claim that ECT is the best route to the target behavior.
- The paper does not address whether the model is using the label as a real world-model key or as a source of truth, which the authors flag as an open failure mode.
